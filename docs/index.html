Your deadlines,
always in view

A small always-on-top window for the Google Calendar events you actually have to do something about.

macOS 14+ Native SwiftUI Liquid Glass Read-only calendar access

What it does

DeadlineFloat sits above your other windows and follows you between Spaces, showing today plus the next two, three or four calendar days — grouped into Overdue, Today, Tomorrow, and then by weekday.

Only the things that are due

Events whose titles start with DUE or SUBMIT, or contain due or deadline. Every keyword is editable, and DONE or CANCELLED items stay hidden.

Real countdowns

“2h 14m left”, updated continuously. All-day events say All day rather than inventing a time, and only go overdue once the day is actually over.

Your Google colours

Exactly the colour Google reports, per event or per calendar. Urgency shows up as an icon, a border and the countdown — never by overwriting the colour you chose.

Never in the way

A non-activating panel: clicking it never steals focus from what you are working in. Drag it anywhere; it remembers where. Adjustable text size and opacity, plus a compact mode.

Correct at the edges

Daylight saving, midnight versus noon, recurring series, multi-day all-day events, duplicates across calendars, offline, and Google's rate limits — all handled and covered by tests.

Out of the way

Lives in the menu bar, no Dock icon. Refreshes every five minutes, on wake from sleep, and whenever the day or time zone changes.

Light, dark, compact

Liquid Glass on macOS 26 and later; a hand-built glass fallback down to macOS 14.

What it can see

  • One Google scope: calendar.readonly. No profile, no email, no other service.
  • Read-only by construction — every Calendar API request must be a GET, enforced in code, so the app cannot create, change or delete an event.
  • Two reachable hosts, both Google's. Every other host is refused before a request leaves your Mac.
  • Tokens live in the macOS Keychain. Calendar data is cached only inside the app's own sandbox container, so the window works offline.
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no server.
